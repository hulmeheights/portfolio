How to price a cleaning job

Every cleaning price comes down to three things: how long the job takes, what your time is worth, and what it costs you to turn up. This calculator estimates the time from the rooms, multiplies it by your hourly rate, then adds products and travel. If the job is small it lifts the price to your minimum charge, and if you are VAT registered it adds 20%.

Start with your hourly rate

Your rate has to cover more than your time on site. As a self-employed cleaner you pay your own tax and National Insurance, get no holiday or sick pay, and spend unpaid time travelling, quoting and replying to messages. If you are not sure what your rate should be, work it out with the hourly rate calculator first. If you pay a helper, their pay must be at least the National Living Wage, which is £12.71 an hour from April 2026 for workers aged 21 and over, so your charge per cleaner hour needs to be well above that.

Get the time right

The time estimate is where most quotes go wrong. The starting times here are a reasonable first guess, but the honest way is to time your next few cleans room by room and put your real total in the time box. A house with pets, clutter or a family of five takes longer than an empty flat with the same number of rooms. For a one-off deep or end of tenancy clean, ask for photos or do a quick visit before you give a fixed price.

Typical ways cleaners structure prices

  • Regular cleans: usually priced by the hour with a minimum of two or three hours a visit. Weekly and fortnightly customers are your steady income, so the calculator shows what each one is worth a month.
  • Deep and end of tenancy cleans: usually a fixed price, with extras like the oven, fridge and inside windows listed separately so the customer can see why the price is what it is.
  • After builders: dust gets everywhere and often needs two visits. Price it as the heaviest job you do.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting travel. A 25 minute drive each way on a two hour clean is nearly an hour of unpaid time. Add it, or set a higher minimum for further jobs.
  • No minimum charge. A one hour job across town can cost you money. A minimum protects you.
  • Pricing by what others charge. Check local prices, but build yours from your own costs, or you may be busy and still short at the end of the month.

Questions

How long does it take to clean a 3 bedroom house?

It depends on the state of the house and how you work. Using this calculator's starting times, a 3 bedroom, 1 bathroom house with one living room comes out at about 2.75 hours for a regular clean, about 5.5 hours for a deep clean and just under 7 hours for an end of tenancy clean before extras like the oven. Time your own cleans and put your real figure in the time box.

Should I charge cleaning by the hour or a fixed price?

Many cleaners charge by the hour for regular cleans, because the customer can see what they are paying for, and a fixed price for one-off deep and end of tenancy cleans, because customers want to know the total before you start. Work the fixed price out from your hourly rate and an honest time estimate, then add travel and products.

Do I have to charge VAT on cleaning?

Only if you are VAT registered. You must register once your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12 months. Below that most sole trader cleaners do not charge VAT. If you are registered, add 20% to your price and show it on the quote and invoice.
