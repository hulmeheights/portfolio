How the hourly rate is worked out

Most self-employed trades undercharge because they divide what they want to earn by 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year. In reality you lose hours to travel, quoting, buying materials and paperwork, and you lose weeks to holidays, bank holidays, illness and quiet spells.

This calculator starts from the money you want in your pocket, adds back the income tax and Class 4 National Insurance you'll pay on your profit, adds your business costs, then spreads the total over the hours you can actually bill.

Typical numbers

  • Billable hours: 25–32 a week is normal for a sole trader once admin and travel are taken out.
  • Working weeks: 44–47. There are 8 bank holidays plus your own holidays.
  • Costs: a van alone can be £4,000–£8,000 a year with insurance, fuel and repairs.

If the number looks high

Check what others in your area charge. If your rate is well above theirs, look at cutting costs or increasing billable hours (for example, quoting by video call instead of driving out). If it's well below what you need, you're paying to work.

Questions

What is a fair hourly rate for a tradesperson in the UK?

It depends on the trade and area, but the rate you need is set by your own costs and the income you want. Use the calculator with your real numbers rather than copying someone else's rate.

Does this include VAT?

No. It assumes you are not VAT registered. If you are, add 20% VAT on top of the rate for customers who are not VAT registered.

Why billable hours and not working hours?

You only get paid for time spent on the job. Travel, quoting, buying materials and paperwork still take time, so they need to be covered by the hours you do bill.
