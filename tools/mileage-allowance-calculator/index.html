How HMRC mileage claims work in 2026/27

If you use your own car or van for work, HMRC lets you claim a flat amount for every business mile instead of adding up fuel, insurance, servicing and wear. For the 2026/27 tax year the rate went up from 45p to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, backdated to 6 April 2026. After 10,000 miles in the tax year it drops to 25p. Motorcycles are 24p a mile. Plenty of guides and spreadsheets still show 45p, so check yours.

Self-employed

Sole traders and partners use these as simplified expenses. The claim comes off your profit, so it reduces both income tax and Class 4 National Insurance. A basic rate taxpayer saves roughly 26p for every pound claimed. The 10,000 mile limit is per vehicle, per tax year, which is why the calculator asks how many miles you have already claimed if you work it out month by month.

Employees

If your employer pays you less than the approved amount, you can claim tax relief on the gap. If they pay more, the extra is taxable. Bicycles at 20p only apply to employees; the self-employed flat rates cover cars, goods vehicles and motorcycles.

Typical numbers

  • A trade doing 120 business miles a week for 46 weeks covers 5,520 miles: £3,036 at 55p, £552 more than the old rate.
  • At 15,000 miles a year: 10,000 at 55p plus 5,000 at 25p, so £6,750.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Claiming fuel as well. Use the mileage rate or actual running costs for a vehicle, never both. Once you use the flat rate for a vehicle, keep using it for as long as you have that vehicle, and you can't use it if you've already claimed capital allowances on it.
  • No log. Keep a record of the date, start and end points, reason and miles for every trip. HMRC can ask for it.
  • Counting commuting. Travel to a fixed workplace you go to every day isn't business mileage. Trips from your base to customers, sites and suppliers usually are.

Questions

Is the HMRC mileage rate 45p or 55p for 2026/27?

55p. HMRC raised the rate for cars and vans from 45p to 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles, backdated to 6 April 2026. Miles over 10,000 in the tax year stay at 25p. Motorcycles are 24p per mile.

Can I claim mileage and fuel for the same van?

No. The mileage rate is meant to cover fuel, insurance, servicing, repairs and wear. If you use it for a vehicle, you do not also claim those running costs or capital allowances for that vehicle. Parking, tolls and congestion charges for business trips can still be claimed on top.

My employer pays less than 55p a mile. Can I claim the difference?

Yes. If your employer pays less than the approved amount, you can claim tax relief on the gap through HMRC. You get back the gap multiplied by your income tax rate, not the full gap. If your job expenses are over £2,500 you claim through Self Assessment.
